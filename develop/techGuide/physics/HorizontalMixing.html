Horizontal Mixing

The Laplacian and biharmonic horizontal mixing of normal velocity and tracers use coefficients that scale with the local mesh size, so that a single set of coefficients suits a mesh whose resolution varies. The options are described in Horizontal Mixing.

Scaling with mesh size

The width of a cell is the diameter of a circle with the same area, and the reference width \(W_{med}\) (MedianCellWidth) is the global median of the cell widths, computed during mesh initialization.

At each edge \(e\), an effective cell width is computed from the mean area of the two adjacent cells \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) (CellsOnEdge):

\[ w_e = 2 \sqrt{\frac{A_{c_1} + A_{c_2}}{2 \pi}}, \]

where \(A_c\) is AreaCell. The Laplacian mixing coefficient (ViscDel2, EddyDiff2) is multiplied by

\[ s^{(2)}_e = \frac{w_e}{W_{med}} \]

(MeshScalingDel2), and the biharmonic coefficient (ViscDel4, EddyDiff4) by

\[ s^{(4)}_e = \left(s^{(2)}_e\right)^3 \]

(MeshScalingDel4). With MeshScalingEnable set to false, both factors are one.

The factors are computed in HorzMesh::computeMeshScaling and HorzMesh::computeMedianCellWidth in src/ocn/HorzMesh.cpp, and applied by the mixing terms in src/ocn/TendencyTerms.cpp.
