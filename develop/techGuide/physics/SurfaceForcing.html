Surface Forcing

How Omega applies the shortwave heating it receives at the surface, and the energy correction it reports to the coupler. The forcing options are described in Forcing, and the implementation in Forcing; both terms are in src/ocn/TendencyTerms.cpp.

Penetrating shortwave radiation

Penetrating shortwave radiation distributes incoming solar heat through the active water column instead of applying all of it to the surface layer. The incoming flux \(I_0\) (ShortWaveHeatFluxCell) is split into near-infrared, red and blue/green bands, each attenuated exponentially with depth. For a cell with surface depth coordinate \(z=0\), the downward flux at depth \(z\) is defined following Manizza et al. (2005) as

\[ I(z) = I_0 \left[ f_{nir} e^{-k_{nir}z} + f_{red} e^{-k_r z} + f_{blue} e^{-k_b z} \right]. \]

Here \(f_{nir}\), \(k_{nir}\), \(f_{red}\), and \(f_{blue}\) are configured with Omega.Tendencies.PenetratingShortwaveTendency.NearIrFraction, Omega.Tendencies.PenetratingShortwaveTendency.NearIrCoeff, Omega.Tendencies.PenetratingShortwaveTendency.RedFraction, and Omega.Tendencies.PenetratingShortwaveTendency.BlueFraction, respectively. Their default values are \(0.58\), \(2.86\ \mathrm{m}^{-1}\), \(0.21\), and \(0.21\), and their sum \(f_{nir} + f_{red} + f_{blue}\) must equal 1.

For each active layer, the deposited heat is the flux at its upper interface minus the flux at its lower interface. For the bottom active layer, the lower interface flux is set to zero. This residual-flux rule makes the vertically integrated deposited heat equal to ShortWaveHeatFluxCell even when the analytic attenuation profile has not reached zero at the model bottom.

Coupled energy correction

The extra energy flux associated with freshwater mass exchange is included in the ocean’s surface heat budget. The coupled system accounts for the corresponding energy as an atmospheric adjustment. This correction is not an additional ocean temperature tendency or a total heat flux.

\[ Q_{\mathrm{extra}} = (F_{\mathrm{rain}} + F_{\mathrm{river}}) h_{\mathrm{fw,in}} + F_{\mathrm{evap}} h_{\mathrm{fw,out}} + (F_{\mathrm{snow}} + F_{\mathrm{iceRunoff}}) (h_{\mathrm{ice}} + L_{\mathrm{ice}}). \]

Here the mass fluxes retain their forcing-array signs, and the specific potential enthalpies are in J kg\(^{-1}\):

  • \(h_{\mathrm{fw,in}} = c^0_{p,sw}\max(C_{T,\mathrm{lim}}, C_{T,\mathrm{top}})\), with Ct0Fw as the limit for TEOS-10 and zero otherwise.

  • \(h_{\mathrm{fw,out}} = c^0_{p,sw} C_{T,\mathrm{top}}\).

  • \(h_{\mathrm{ice}} = -L_{\mathrm{ice}}\) in the current approximation, so the snow and ice-runoff contribution to this correction is currently zero.

Direct heat fluxes and sea-ice meltwater enthalpy are explicitly accounted for in the coupling fluxes and are not added to this correction.
